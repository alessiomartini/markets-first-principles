---
term: "Beta"
aliases: ["market beta", "β"]
track: 4
status: written
---

The sensitivity of an asset's or portfolio's return to the return of a reference, usually the market, estimated as the covariance between the two divided by the variance of the reference: $\beta = \mathrm{Cov}(r, r_m)/\mathrm{Var}(r_m)$. A beta of 1.2 means that, on average, the asset has moved 1.2% for every 1% move in the market. It is the slope of a regression, so it is an estimate with an error, and it is not constant: it shifts across periods and tends to rise when markets fall. Return that comes from beta can be obtained cheaply by holding the market, which is why portfolio managers separate it from the part of return that is specific to them.
