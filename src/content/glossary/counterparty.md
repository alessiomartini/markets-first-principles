The person or institution on the other side of your trade — the one who owes you something if the contract says so. On an exchange you generally never learn their name, because a clearing house interposes itself between the two sides immediately after the match, so that each of you faces the clearer instead of each other. That substitution is the whole reason anonymous trading is possible. Where no clearer exists, as in most over-the-counter markets, the identity of your counterparty is part of the asset: the same contract with a weaker counterparty is genuinely worth less, and that difference has a price.
