---
term: "Curve inversion"
aliases: ["inverted yield curve", "inverted curve"]
track: 5
status: written
---

A state in which short-dated yields are higher than long-dated ones, so the yield curve slopes downward. It usually reflects the central bank holding the **policy rate** high while markets expect it to be cut, because growth or inflation is expected to weaken. Inversions of the 10-year against the 3-month or 2-year yield have preceded most US recessions since the 1960s, but the sample is small, the lead time varies from months to two years, and the cause is debated, so it is a signal rather than a mechanism.
