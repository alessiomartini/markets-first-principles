A correction of a backtest's Sharpe ratio proposed by Bailey and Lopez de Prado (2014), giving the probability that the true Sharpe ratio is positive after allowing for the number of strategies tried, the sample length and the skewness and kurtosis of returns. The best of 100 skill-free rules over five years can be expected to show a Sharpe ratio near 1.1, so a headline number means little without the count of attempts.
