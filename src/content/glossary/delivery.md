The actual handover of the asset that a contract promises. For a share it is a book entry; for a barrel of oil it is a cargo at a named terminal in a named month. Most derivative contracts are closed out before delivery and settle in cash instead, but the *possibility* of delivery is what anchors the contract's price to the physical world — and when the deliverable supply is small relative to open positions, that anchor becomes a squeeze. Negative oil prices in April 2020 happened because holders of expiring contracts had nowhere to put the barrels.
