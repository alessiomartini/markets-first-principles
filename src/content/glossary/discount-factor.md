The number you multiply a future payment by to get its **present value**: $D(t,T)$ for a payment at $T$ seen from $t$. Working with discount factors rather than rates is the practitioner's habit, because they multiply cleanly across periods and because they are what the market actually quotes once you strip away conventions. A discount factor above one means a negative rate, which used to be considered impossible and was normal in Europe for most of a decade.
