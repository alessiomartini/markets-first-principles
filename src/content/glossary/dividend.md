A cash payment made to shareholders out of profits. A dividend does not create value — the share price falls by roughly the amount paid on the ex-dividend date, since the company now holds less cash — which is why total return rather than price return is the only honest measure of what an equity investor earned. Dividends matter enormously over long horizons: over decades they account for a large share of cumulative equity returns.
