A distribution has fat tails when the probability of large outcomes decays as a power of their size rather than exponentially, so extreme events are far more likely than a Gaussian would allow. For financial returns this is not a subtle correction: at four standard deviations the empirical probability sits orders of magnitude above the Gaussian one, and the difference grows further out. The practical consequence is that the quantities anyone actually cares about — the worst **drawdown**, the depth of a **VaR breach**, the capital needed to survive — are dominated by the tail rather than by the body of the distribution, so a model fitted to the body will be confidently wrong about all of them. The tail's shape is summarised by the **tail exponent**, which for equity returns sits near 3 across markets and decades.
