term: "Fixed income"
aliases: ["bond market", "rates", "debt"]

Claims that promise a defined schedule of payments: government and corporate bonds, bills, notes, loans, and the derivatives on them. The name is a mild lie — floating-rate instruments sit in the same asset class and their payments are not fixed — but the organising idea holds, which is that the cash flows are contractually specified rather than residual. That makes the two questions that matter unusually sharp: what discount rate applies to those payments, and will they arrive at all. Fixed income is far larger than equity by amount outstanding, and it is where central bank policy transmits first.
