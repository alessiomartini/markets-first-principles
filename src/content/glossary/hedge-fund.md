A pooled investment vehicle, open to professional investors, that faces few constraints on what it may hold: it can go short, use leverage, and concentrate. The name is now almost entirely historical — most hedge funds do not hedge in any meaningful sense — and the category spans strategies with nothing in common beyond a fee structure. What unites them operationally is that they are the marginal buyer of risk in most markets, and that their leverage makes them forced sellers when it is withdrawn.
