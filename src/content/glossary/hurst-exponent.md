---
term: "Hurst exponent"
aliases: ["Hurst", "H"]

physics: "The scaling exponent of a self-affine process, identical in definition to the roughness exponent of a fractal surface."
---

The exponent $H$ in $\mathbb{E}|r_\tau| \sim \tau^H$, describing how the typical size of a move grows with the horizon. A memoryless random walk gives $H = 1/2$; $H > 1/2$ means trending, $H < 1/2$ mean-reverting. Measured values for financial prices sit very close to $1/2$, which is the quantitative statement that prices are hard to predict — and the honest caveat is that $H$ estimates are notoriously biased in short samples, so small deviations from a half should be treated with suspicion.
