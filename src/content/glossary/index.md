A published, rule-based measure of a set of securities, such as the S&P 500, which is weighted by the market value of the shares available to the public. An index is a calculation and cannot itself be bought; investors hold a fund that replicates it, with fees and a small tracking difference. Its rules matter because large amounts of money follow them, so inclusion or exclusion moves prices.
