---
term: "Inflation expectations"
aliases: ["expected inflation"]
track: 5
status: written
---

What households, firms and markets believe inflation will be over some future horizon. They matter because wages, contracts and prices are set on the basis of them, so an expectation of higher inflation can raise actual inflation; this is why central banks put so much weight on keeping expectations *anchored* near their target. They are measured by surveys and by markets: the gap between a nominal government bond yield and an inflation-linked one of the same maturity is the *breakeven* rate, which mixes expected inflation with a premium for its uncertainty and some liquidity effects.
