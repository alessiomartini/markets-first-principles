---
term: "Kelly criterion"
aliases: ["Kelly bet", "Kelly fraction"]
track: 4
status: written
---

The rule for the fraction of capital to stake on a repeated bet with positive expected value so as to maximise the long-run growth rate of wealth, which is the same as maximising the expected logarithm of wealth. For a simple bet with win probability $p$ and net odds $b$ the fraction is $f^* = (bp - (1-p))/b$. Betting more than $f^*$ lowers the long-run growth and increases drawdowns, and betting twice $f^*$ gives zero growth. Because the edge and the odds are only estimated, practitioners usually bet a fraction of Kelly, such as half. See **risk of ruin**.
