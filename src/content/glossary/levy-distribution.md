The family of distributions that are stable under addition — the sum of independent copies has the same shape, rescaled — with the Gaussian as the special case of finite variance. Lévy distributions with $\alpha < 2$ have infinite variance and were the first serious proposal, by Mandelbrot in the 1960s, for the fat tails of returns. The modern verdict is that they are the right *idea* and the wrong *fit*: measured tail exponents cluster around 3, outside the stable range, so returns are fat-tailed but do have finite variance.
