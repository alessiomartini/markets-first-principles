The ease with which a position can be turned into cash without moving the price against you. The word covers three distinct measurable things that often move in opposite directions: the **bid-ask spread** (what a small trade costs), **depth** (how much size is available near the touch), and *resilience* (how quickly the book refills after being hit). A market can be tight and shallow, or wide and deep, and calling both "liquid" hides exactly the information you need. Liquidity is also not a constant of the asset: it collapses precisely when everyone wants it, which is what makes it a risk rather than a convenience.
