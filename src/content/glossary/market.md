A market is any arrangement in which prospective buyers and sellers discover the terms on which they will trade. It needs no building, no screen, and not even continuous operation: a weekly livestock auction, a phone call between two bank desks, and a matching engine processing a million messages a second are all markets. What distinguishes one from another is the *mechanism* — who is allowed to participate, how they express interest, how a match is made, and what gets published afterwards. Almost every practical question about a market ("why is the price different here?", "why did it gap?", "why can I not sell?") turns out to be a question about its mechanism rather than about the asset being traded.
