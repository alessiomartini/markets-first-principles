The asset chosen as the unit in which other prices are measured, so that every price becomes a ratio to it. The choice is arbitrary but not innocent: a **risk-neutral measure** is defined relative to a numéraire, and the measure changes when the numéraire does. Using the money-market account gives the usual risk-neutral measure; using a zero-coupon bond or the underlying itself can make a pricing problem much simpler, because the quantity that must be a martingale is then a different ratio. The price of a derivative itself does not depend on the choice.
