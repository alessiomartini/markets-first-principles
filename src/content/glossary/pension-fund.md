A pool of assets set aside to meet future retirement payments. What makes pension funds distinctive is that they have *liabilities* with a known shape — payments stretching decades ahead — so their risk is the gap between assets and the present value of those promises, not the volatility of the assets alone. This makes them structural buyers of long-dated **fixed income**, and it means a fall in interest rates can leave a fund worse off despite every asset it owns having gone up.
