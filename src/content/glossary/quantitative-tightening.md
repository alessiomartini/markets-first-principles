The reverse of **quantitative easing**: the central bank shrinks its balance sheet by letting the bonds it holds mature without reinvesting the proceeds, or occasionally by selling them. Reserves in the banking system fall as a result, and the duration the private sector must hold rises, which tends to push long yields up through the **term premium**. Because the effects are known less well than those of easing, it is usually done slowly and announced in advance.
