Tangible, non-financial holdings: property, infrastructure, farmland, timber, and sometimes precious metals. They are grouped together not by what they are but by how they behave in a portfolio — illiquid, valued by appraisal rather than by continuous trading, and expected to hold value against inflation because their cash flows are rents on physical things. That appraisal-based valuation makes their reported volatility misleadingly low: a price that is only estimated quarterly cannot move sharply, which is a property of the measurement rather than of the risk.
