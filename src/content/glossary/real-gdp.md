Gross domestic product measured at the prices of a fixed base period, so that changes reflect the quantity of goods and services produced and not their price. It is obtained by dividing **nominal GDP** by a price index, the **GDP deflator**. Growth in real GDP is the usual definition of economic growth, and its gap from the level the economy could sustain is the *output gap*. Real GDP is estimated, revised for years afterwards, and depends on how statisticians treat quality change, so it is less precise than the single figure suggests.
