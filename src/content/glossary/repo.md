A sale of a security combined with an agreement to buy it back at a fixed later date and price. Economically it is a loan against collateral: the seller receives cash now, the buyer holds the security as protection, and the difference between the two prices is the interest, quoted as the repo rate. Repo is the main way securities dealers and many funds finance their positions, and the base of short-term funding in the financial system. Its stability depends on the collateral keeping its value, which is why lenders apply a margin or haircut and why the market can seize up when collateral values are in doubt.
