Whether the asset can be held from one period to the next, and at what cost. Storability is the single property that most changes how a price behaves, because it lets arbitrage connect today's price to expected future prices: if the forward price exceeds the **spot** price by more than the cost of storing and financing the asset, someone buys spot, stores, and sells forward. Where storage is impossible the link breaks — electricity cannot be warehoused, so its price can spike a hundredfold for an hour and mean nothing for tomorrow.
