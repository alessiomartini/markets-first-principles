---
term: "Tail correlation"
aliases: ["tail dependence", "correlation in the tails"]
track: 4
status: written
---

The tendency of assets to fall together in extreme events even when their ordinary day-to-day correlation is low. A single correlation coefficient averages over all days and is dominated by ordinary ones, so it can understate how dependent assets become in a crash. Measured only on the worst days, correlations are generally much higher, which is the reason diversification tends to fail exactly when it is needed. This is a central reason why risk estimates built from normal-times correlations, including **value at risk**, understate portfolio losses in a crisis.
