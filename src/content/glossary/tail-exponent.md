The exponent $\alpha$ in $P(|r| > x) \sim x^{-\alpha}$, describing how quickly the probability of large returns decays. It is the sharpest single summary of tail risk, because it says which moments of the distribution exist: moments of order $\alpha$ and above are infinite. Empirically $\alpha$ sits near 3 to 4 for equity returns across markets and decades — the *inverse cubic law* — which means variance exists but kurtosis is borderline, and it is why estimates of kurtosis are so unstable in practice.
