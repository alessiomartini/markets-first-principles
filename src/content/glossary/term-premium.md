---
term: "Term premium"
aliases: ["bond risk premium", "duration premium"]
track: 5
status: written
---

The extra yield that investors require to hold a long-maturity bond instead of rolling over short-maturity ones, as compensation for bearing the risk that rates and inflation move against them. A long yield can be decomposed into the average expected short rate over its life plus this premium. It is not directly observable: it is estimated from models, and different models give different answers, so claims that it is high or negative should be read with that in mind. It tends to fall when central banks buy long bonds, as in **quantitative easing**.
