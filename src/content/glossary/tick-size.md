---

The smallest permitted price increment in a market. It sets a floor on the **bid-ask spread**, and where that floor binds — a stock whose natural spread would be narrower than one tick — competition moves from price to **queue priority**, since makers can no longer undercut each other and must instead race to be earlier in the line. Tick size is thus a policy lever that determines whether a market rewards better prices or faster machines.
