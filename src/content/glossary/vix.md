An index published by Cboe that measures the 30-day **implied volatility** of the S&P 500, computed from the prices of a strip of out-of-the-money index options across many strikes rather than from a single Black-Scholes inversion. It is quoted in annualised percentage points: a VIX of 20 means the options market prices an S&P 500 move of about $20/\sqrt{12} \approx 5.8\%$ over the next month, one standard deviation. The VIX is not directly tradable, but futures, options and exchange-traded products are written on it. It tends to rise when equity prices fall, which is why it is called the fear index, and it is a risk-neutral quantity that typically exceeds subsequently realised volatility.
