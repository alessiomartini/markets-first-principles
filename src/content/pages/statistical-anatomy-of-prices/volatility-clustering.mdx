## IN ONE SENTENCE

Whether the price goes up or down tomorrow is nearly unforecastable, but whether tomorrow is a violent day or a calm
one is highly forecastable.

## WHY IT EXISTS

Two forces pull in opposite directions and both win, in different variables.

The sign of a return is competed away. Any reliable pattern in direction is a trade, and trading it removes it, so
the autocorrelation of returns is pushed toward zero by arbitrage. This is the defensible core of the **efficient
market hypothesis**, and empirically it holds tightly beyond a few minutes.

The magnitude is not competed away, because knowing that tomorrow will be volatile is not by itself a directional
trade. Nothing removes it, and several mechanisms sustain it: information arrives in bursts, large orders are split
across days, losses force deleveraging which causes more selling, and market makers widen quotes when uncertain,
which amplifies moves. The result is that variance persists while direction does not.

## DEFINITION

**Heteroskedasticity** is variance that changes over time. In returns it is not a nuisance but the dominant feature:
volatility varies by an order of magnitude between calm and stressed periods, and does so persistently enough to
forecast.

**Realised volatility** is volatility computed from observed returns over a window. Unlike the mean, it converges
as sampling gets finer, because it estimates a quadratic variation — until microstructure noise takes over and you
begin measuring the **bid-ask spread** bouncing rather than the price moving.

**GARCH** is the workhorse model: today's variance depends on yesterday's variance and yesterday's squared return.
Three parameters capture clustering well enough to be the industry standard.

**Intermittency** is the qualitative face of the phenomenon — activity concentrated in bursts, so most of a year's
variance arrives on a handful of days. **Long memory** is its quantitative face: the autocorrelation of absolute
returns decays as a power law rather than exponentially, so there is no horizon beyond which volatility forgets.

## FORMALLY

The two facts, stated side by side, are the content of the page. For returns,

$$
C_r(\tau) = \operatorname{corr}\left(r_t, r_{t+\tau}\right) \approx 0 \quad \text{for } \tau \gtrsim \text{minutes}
$$

while for their magnitudes,

$$
C_{|r|}(\tau) = \operatorname{corr}\left(|r_t|, |r_{t+\tau}|\right) \sim \tau^{-\nu}, \qquad \nu \approx 0.2
$$

with the power law holding over lags from days to months. An exponent that small means the decay is extraordinarily
slow: the sum $\sum_\tau C_{|r|}(\tau)$ diverges, so the process has no finite correlation time and no window is
long enough to average the dependence away.

**GARCH(1,1)** captures the clustering but not this. Writing

$$
\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \alpha r_{t-1}^2 + \beta \sigma_{t-1}^2
$$

gives an autocorrelation decaying as $(\alpha + \beta)^\tau$ — exponential, with a characteristic time. Fitted
values of $\alpha + \beta$ come out close to $0.99$ precisely because the estimator is straining to mimic a power
law with an exponential, and that near-unit-root is a symptom of model misspecification rather than a discovery
about markets.

## PHYSICS BRIDGE

<PhysicsBridge idea="Intermittency and multiplicative cascades">
This is the same phenomenon as intermittency in turbulence, and the correspondence is not loose. In a turbulent
flow, energy dissipation is not spread evenly but concentrated in rare violent regions, and the statistics are
generated by a multiplicative cascade transferring energy from large scales to small ones. In markets, volatility
is not spread evenly but concentrated in bursts, and the models that fit best are multiplicative cascades
transferring "activity" from long time scales to short ones.

The reason the analogy is productive rather than decorative is that it predicts what GARCH gets wrong. A cascade
generates power-law memory automatically, because the mechanism is scale-free by construction; an autoregressive
model with fixed lags generates exponential memory and must be pushed to the edge of stationarity to fake anything
slower. The multifractal random walk, which is literally a cascade construction borrowed from turbulence,
reproduces the observed $\tau^{-0.2}$ decay with no near-unit-root pathology — and it is the subject of the next
page.

## COMMON CONFUSIONS

- **Predictable volatility is not predictable returns.** Knowing tomorrow is risky tells you how much to size, not
  which way to bet.
- **Clustering is not a trend in volatility.** Volatility is strongly mean-reverting; it is the *slowness* of that
  reversion, not its absence, that constitutes the memory.
- **A GARCH fit near $\alpha + \beta = 1$ is not evidence of a unit root in variance.** It is what an exponential
  model does when the truth is a power law, and it is a warning about the model rather than a fact about markets.
- **Clustering is not caused by news arriving in clusters alone.** Volatility persists far longer than any
  identifiable news cycle, and it appears in markets with no news at all.
